# sft-v0: first SFT of Qwen3.8-27B on COBOL, compared with the frozen baseline

## 1. Summary

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Did the training run work? | **Yes.** QLoRA r 16 on 712 rows, 135 steps (3 epochs), 26.2 GB peak, eval loss 0.217 → 0.175 and flat over the last epoch (no overfitting). The run is restartable (resume from W&B checked), and the adapter loads and is applied in vLLM (after a fix, section 6). |
| Does the adapted model write better COBOL on the benchmarks, under the frozen protocol? | **No.** COBOLEval pass@1 falls (greedy 0.043 → 0.000, sampled 0.019 → 0.003, both outside the 95% CI) and HumanEval-Python sampled pass@1 falls by 3.4 points (the success criterion allowed 2). CobolCodeBench completion sampled rises (0.008 → 0.020, pass@10 0.048 → 0.083), but its interval just touches 0. |
| Why? | Two format artifacts, not lack of COBOL knowledge (section 5): the first line of the program loses its indentation (a prompt-building defect that the fine-tuned model copies and the baseline repairs), and `STOP RUN`, which is in 99% of the training programs, ends a COBOLEval subprogram before it returns. With the first-line indentation restored the compile rate of sft-v0 on COBOLEval goes from 0.121 (baseline) to 0.646 (greedy); with `STOP RUN` also rewritten to `GOBACK` its greedy pass@1 is 0.322 against 0.062 for the baseline (post hoc, section 5). |
| What is the evidence that SFT worked at all? | Compile rate under the indentation fix (+0.52 greedy), pass@1 +0.26 and pass@10 +0.31 on COBOLEval when both format artifacts are removed (post hoc), eval loss on tasks held out of training, the logprob shift of the served adapter. All are indirect: we have **no test on tasks of the training distribution that is free of selection bias** (section 7). |
| Decision | Do not ship sft-v0. Next: fix the prompt and extraction artifacts, build training data in the benchmark formats (LINKAGE, files, `GOBACK`), then retrain (`sft-v1`). |

## 2. Setup

- **Base model:** Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B, text only, non-thinking mode. QLoRA 4-bit; LoRA r 16, alpha 32, dropout 0.05 on the attention, Gated DeltaNet and MLP projections (116 727 808 trainable parameters).
- **Training data:** `sft-dataset:v0` (alias `decontaminated`), 811 rows on 349 tasks: 328 programs generated by the base model and kept only if they compile with GnuCOBOL 3.2 and pass the tests (rejection sampling, 584 passing samples out of 40 816), and 483 human CodeNet COBOL submissions. 0 n-gram matches with COBOLEval and CobolCodeBench. Split by task: 712 train rows, 99 eval rows.
- **What the data looks like:** competition programs that read stdin with `ACCEPT` and print with `DISPLAY` (99% `ACCEPT`, 100% `DISPLAY`); 0 of 811 have a `LINKAGE SECTION`, 51 touch files, 799 contain `STOP RUN`, 1 contains `GOBACK`.
- **Training:** lr 2e-4 cosine, batch 1 × accumulation 16, `max_length` 4096 (nothing dropped), 3 epochs, one checkpoint and eval per epoch, 15.2 s per step, 1× RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell. Code commit `9c4da41`.
- **Serving and evaluation:** vLLM 0.32.0, base and adapter (`cobol`) side by side. Same protocol as the frozen baseline (`configs/eval/sft-v0.yaml` differs from `baseline.yaml` only in run name, adapter, served model and mode; a test checks it): COBOLEval, CobolCodeBench completion and instruct, HumanEval-Python; greedy and sampled (n 20, temperature 0.7); 9246 generations, the same group sizes as the baseline. Execution in the GnuCOBOL 3.2.0 sandbox (no network, read-only). Non-thinking only.
- **Artifacts and runs:** [training run](https://wandb.ai/mllab-team/onprem-codegen/runs/sft-v0) (`sft-v0-adapter:candidate`, `sft-v0-ckpt` step 44/88/132/135), [generation run](https://wandb.ai/mllab-team/onprem-codegen/runs/o47hz4el) (`eval-generations:sft-v0`), [execution run](https://wandb.ai/mllab-team/onprem-codegen/runs/ke91f26o) (`eval-results:sft-v0`). Baseline: `docs/baseline.md`, aliases `baseline-final-*`.

## 5. Why COBOLEval fell: two format artifacts

All numbers below are **exploratory, post hoc and local** (no W&B, generations unchanged, only the text of the program is edited before execution). They do not replace the frozen results above.

**Artifact 1: first-line indentation.** The COBOLEval prompt starts with seven spaces. `build_prompt` calls `task_text.strip()` ([prompt.py:22](../src/onprem_codegen/prompt.py#L22)), so the model sees `IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.` in column 1. Fixed-format COBOL needs the first line indented, so a copy of it fails with `invalid indicator 'F' at column 7`. The baseline repairs the indentation by itself (402 of 438 greedy answers start indented); sft-v0 copies the prompt literally (0 of 438). This is 417 of the 423 greedy compile errors.

**Artifact 2: `STOP RUN` from the training data.** 799 of the 811 training programs contain `STOP RUN`; sft-v0 writes it in 84% of the greedy COBOLEval programs and never writes `GOBACK` (baseline: 33% and 37 programs). COBOLEval calls the program as a subprogram, where `STOP RUN` ends the run before the result is read: 264 of the 271 wrong outputs after fixing artifact 1 contain `STOP RUN`.

COBOLEval, local re-execution of the stored generations (146 tasks):

| Indentation restored + `STOP RUN` → `GOBACK` | pass@10, sampled | 0.300 | **0.608** | +0.308 [+0.240, +0.373] |

With both artifacts removed, sft-v0 is **far better** than the baseline on COBOLEval (greedy pass@1 0.062 → 0.322, 141 programs correct against 27; sampled pass@10 0.300 → 0.608), and the interval of every difference is clearly above 0. The sign of the result flips: what limited sft-v0 on COBOLEval was the format of the program (how the first line is indented, how the program ends), not missing COBOL knowledge.

**This is an explanation, not a result.** The two edits were chosen after the failure was seen, and `STOP RUN` → `GOBACK` rewrites the model's own answer, which nobody can do at deployment. Both models were edited the same way (the baseline gains too: greedy 0.043 → 0.062), but the headline result of sft-v0 stays the frozen-protocol table in section 4. What the numbers do show is that a model trained on the same data with the program ending fixed in the training data and the prompt fixed in the evaluation (the plan for `sft-v1`) has a large effect to gain on COBOLEval, and that the cost of a wrong format is larger than the effect of the training itself.

The baseline itself also gains from the indentation fix (sampled pass@1 0.019 → 0.030), so the frozen baseline understates the base model on COBOLEval. This is a defect of the shared prompt, not a favour to one model.

## 6. Serving the adapter: a silent failure that was found and fixed

With the adapter as trained, vLLM loaded it without a message, `serve.check` said both models answer, but `cobol` was **identical** to `base` (the sum of token logprobs of a fixed COBOL text was −66.002127 for both). Cause: the text-only trainer saves keys `…model.layers.N…`, vLLM looks LoRA weights up under `…model.language_model.layers.N…`, so none of the 496 LoRA modules matched and nothing was applied. `serve/adapter_keys.py` renames the keys (496 of 496 then match the base checkpoint) and `serve.check` now fails when the adapter has no effect on the logprobs (after the fix: −66.0021 vs −62.9725). Any earlier LoRA served this way would have looked like a null result.

## 7. Limits of this report

- **One training run, one seed, one configuration.** No sweep, no repeat, so run-to-run noise of the training is unknown. The intervals above only cover the sampling of tasks.
- **Non-thinking only.** Thinking was measured for the baseline on COBOLEval and HumanEval-Python only; adapted thinking was not run (about 15 GPU hours).
- **The diagnostics are post hoc.** They show where the drop comes from but were chosen after seeing the results; treat the restored-indentation and `GOBACK` numbers as an explanation, not as a result.
- **No unbiased in-distribution test.** `task-pool` was filtered through the baseline: only 19 of the 1755 tasks outside the training set had a single passing base sample, so a fresh comparison on them is biased against the base model, and the 99 dev rows (35 tasks) are biased towards it. An unbiased test needs a random split of the pool before any filtering.
- **Training data and benchmarks differ in form** (stdin programs against LINKAGE and file programs), a known and deliberate choice for v0 (project log, "SFT prompt format vs benchmark format"); the results show it matters.
- **CobolCodeBench has 46 tasks per mode**, so its intervals are wide, and its truncation increase is a regression of its own.
- COBOLEval prompt defect and `max_new_tokens` stay unchanged here to keep the comparison with the frozen baseline valid.

## 8. Next steps

1. **Prompt and extraction:** keep the indentation of the first line in `build_prompt`; decide whether to re-freeze the baseline (re-execution of the stored generations is enough for an extraction fix, new generations for a prompt fix).
2. **Data in the benchmark formats:** programs with `LINKAGE SECTION` ending in `GOBACK`, programs with files, from `clean-corpus` (4804 real COBOL files, not used yet), validated by compilation and tests.
3. **Unbiased in-distribution test:** random split of `task-pool` before rejection sampling.
4. **Retrain (`sft-v1`)** with the new data and a Python-regularising mix; sweep only if v1 shows a gain.
5. **Thinking evaluation** of the final model if the budget allows.
